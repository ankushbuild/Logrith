Frequently Asked Questions

What is a standard tip percentage?

Tip customs vary by country — 15-20% is common in the US for restaurant service, while other regions have different norms or include service charges automatically.

Can I split the bill unevenly?

Yes, switch to "Uneven Split" mode and enter each person's individual bill share — the tip is calculated proportionally.

Is the tip split equally in uneven mode too?

Each person's tip is calculated as the same percentage of their own share, so someone with a larger share pays a proportionally larger tip.

Tip Calculator and Bill Splitter

This tool calculates the tip on a restaurant bill and splits the total among a group, supporting both a simple equal split and a more flexible uneven split for situations where people ordered different amounts.

Equal Split vs. Uneven Split

Equal split simply divides the bill plus tip evenly among everyone in the group — the simplest and most common approach when everyone ordered roughly similar amounts. Uneven split lets you enter each person's individual portion of the bill, then calculates their proportional share of the tip based on what they actually ordered — a fairer approach when there's a significant difference between what different people spent, such as one person ordering drinks and appetizers while another had just a small dish.

How Tip Percentage Is Calculated

The tip amount is simply the bill total multiplied by the tip percentage. For an equal split, this combined bill-plus-tip total is then divided evenly by the number of people. For an uneven split, each person's individual bill amount is used to calculate their own proportional tip, and their final share is their bill amount plus their calculated tip.

Common Tipping Practices

  • 15-20% is a common range for restaurant service in the United States.
  • Some countries already include a service charge in the bill, making additional tipping optional or unusual.
  • Group dining often defaults to splitting the tip evenly, even when the bill itself is split unevenly, though this calculator supports proportional tip splitting for a fairer approach.

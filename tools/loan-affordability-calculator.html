Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI-to-income ratio?

It's the percentage of your monthly income that goes toward loan EMIs. Lenders typically consider 40-50% a safe maximum, including all existing debt obligations.

Why does existing EMI matter?

Lenders assess your total EMI burden across all loans, not just the new one. Existing EMIs reduce how much additional EMI — and therefore loan amount — you can safely take on.

Is this the same as what a bank will approve?

This gives a reasonable estimate based on standard affordability principles, but actual bank approval also depends on credit score, employment type, and the specific lender's policies.

How Much Loan Can You Afford?

This calculator estimates the maximum loan amount you can reasonably afford based on your monthly income, existing debt obligations, and a target EMI-to-income ratio — the reverse of a standard EMI calculation. Instead of asking "what will my EMI be for this loan amount," it asks "what's the largest loan I can take on while keeping my EMI within a safe percentage of my income."

What Is a Safe EMI-to-Income Ratio?

Most lenders and financial advisors use a guideline where total EMI obligations (across all loans combined) shouldn't exceed roughly 40–50% of monthly income. Staying below this threshold leaves enough income for living expenses, savings, and unexpected costs, while reducing the risk of financial strain if income drops or expenses rise unexpectedly. This calculator lets you adjust this ratio to see how a more conservative or more aggressive target changes your maximum affordable loan.

How This Tool Calculates Your Maximum Loan

The tool first determines your maximum affordable EMI by multiplying your monthly income by your chosen EMI-to-income ratio, then subtracting any existing EMI obligations you already have. This gives the EMI amount available for a new loan. From there, it works the standard EMI formula in reverse — using your chosen interest rate and tenure — to calculate the largest loan principal that would produce exactly that EMI amount.

Why This Matters Before Applying for a Loan

Knowing your realistic affordability range before applying for a home loan, car loan, or personal loan helps you shop within a sensible budget, avoid over-borrowing, and negotiate from an informed position. It also helps you understand the trade-off between loan tenure and affordability — a longer tenure lowers the EMI for the same loan amount, which can increase what you're able to borrow, at the cost of paying more total interest over time.

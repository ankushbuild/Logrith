Frequently Asked Questions

Why add a watermark to an image?

Watermarks help protect ownership of photos and graphics shared online, discourage unauthorized reuse, and add branding to shared images.

Will the watermark reduce image quality?

No. The watermark text is drawn on top of the original image at full resolution — the underlying photo quality is unaffected.

Why Watermark Your Images?

Watermarking is a simple, low-effort way to protect creative work shared publicly online. Photographers, designers, and content creators commonly add a subtle watermark — a name, logo text, or website URL — to portfolio previews, social media posts, and stock samples, making it harder for others to claim the work as their own or use it without permission. A watermark doesn't prevent copying outright, but it does make casual misuse more visible and less appealing, and it reinforces attribution wherever the image circulates.

Choosing the Right Watermark Settings

  • Position: corner placements (bottom-right is most common) keep the watermark unobtrusive while still visible; center placement is bolder and harder to crop out.
  • Tiled mode: repeats the watermark across the entire image in a grid, making it very difficult to remove by cropping — useful for preview images you don't want reused at all.
  • Opacity: lower opacity (20–40%) keeps the watermark subtle and less distracting from the image itself; higher opacity makes it more prominent and harder to ignore or edit out.
  • Font size: scale relative to your image dimensions — a watermark that's too small is easy to crop away, while one that's too large distracts from the image content.

How This Tool Works

Once you upload an image, it's drawn onto an HTML canvas at its original resolution. Your watermark text is then rendered on top using the settings you choose — position, size, color, and opacity — using the canvas's native text and transparency rendering. The final combined image is exported as a PNG file you can download immediately, with the watermark permanently baked into the pixel data.

Privacy and Local Processing

All watermarking happens locally in your browser using the Canvas API. Your original image is never uploaded to a server, keeping unpublished photos and designs completely private until you choose to share them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy BMI range?

A BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is generally considered healthy for most adults, per WHO classification.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a useful screening tool but doesn't account for muscle mass or body composition, so it may be less accurate for athletes, elderly people, or pregnant women.

How is BMI calculated?

BMI = weight in kg ÷ (height in meters)². For imperial units, BMI = 703 × weight in lb ÷ (height in inches)².

Free BMI Calculator — Metric & Imperial

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a widely used screening measure that estimates whether a person's weight is healthy relative to their height. This calculator computes your BMI instantly in either metric (kilograms and centimeters) or imperial (pounds and feet/inches) units, and shows which of the four standard WHO categories your result falls into: underweight, normal weight, overweight, or obese.

How BMI Is Calculated

In metric units, BMI is calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m²). In imperial units, the formula is adjusted with a conversion factor: 703 × weight in pounds, divided by height in inches squared. Both formulas produce the same BMI value for the same person — they're just different ways of expressing the same underlying weight-to-height-squared ratio.

Understanding BMI Categories

  • Below 18.5 — Underweight: may indicate insufficient nutrition or an underlying health condition.
  • 18.5–24.9 — Normal weight: generally associated with the lowest health risk for most adults.
  • 25–29.9 — Overweight: associated with increased risk of certain health conditions.
  • 30 and above — Obese: associated with significantly increased health risk, often further subdivided into Class I, II, and III obesity.

Limitations of BMI

BMI is a useful, quick screening tool precisely because it only requires height and weight, but it doesn't distinguish between muscle mass and fat mass. A muscular athlete can have a "high" BMI while carrying very little body fat, while someone with low muscle mass and high body fat could have a "normal" BMI despite carrying unhealthy levels of fat. BMI also doesn't account for fat distribution, age-related changes, or differences between individuals of different body frames. It's best used as a general population-level screening indicator rather than a definitive individual health diagnosis — a doctor or health professional can provide more complete context using additional measurements.

Healthy Weight Range

Alongside your BMI number, this tool calculates the weight range that would put you in the "normal" BMI category (18.5–24.9) at your current height. This can be a more actionable number than the BMI value itself, since it translates the BMI formula back into a concrete weight target in your usual units.
